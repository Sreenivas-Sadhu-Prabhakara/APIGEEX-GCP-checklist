Concept

Apigee X in plain terms

A managed front door for every digital service you expose — to partners, mobile apps, internal teams and AI agents. Security, traffic control and analytics are enforced once, consistently.

API — a contract that lets one system safely use another's data or function.
Proxy — Apigee's managed façade in front of each backend; you evolve the proxy, not the backend.
Policy — a reusable rule (auth, quota, mediation) attached to a proxy to enforce behaviour without code.
Speaker notes: Use the “reception desk” analogy: checks ID (auth), enforces appointment limits (quota / spike arrest), translates languages (mediation), keeps a visitor log (analytics). Stress that backends don't change — the proxy absorbs it. A “policy” is the unit of enforcement, configured not coded.

Business value

How Apigee is organized

The Apigee X object model

Four concepts shape everything downstream — routing, promotion, residency and cost. Worth agreeing early.

Organization

Bound 1:1 to the project; its control-plane region and subscription are effectively permanent. apigee.googleapis.com/organizations

Env Group ↔ hostname

Determines DNS & load-balancer routing — design before building ingress.

Instance ↔ Environment

Instances are regional compute; environments are deployment scopes attached to them. Multi-region = attach to 2+ instances.

Speaker notes: This model drives later decisions: env groups → DNS/LB hostnames; environments → promotion model and config-as-code; instances → regions, HA and cost. The org's region and subscription tier can't be changed later without re-provisioning, so lock them in discovery.

Reference Architecture

The “Landing Zone Blueprint” for Apigee X

Indicative enterprise topology. Each numbered zone maps to a checklist slide that follows.

Speaker notes: Walk left→right: consumer hits a public hostname (DNS) → Global External ALB on 443 with a managed cert and Cloud Armor → a PSC NEG privately reaches the Apigee runtime's service attachment (no public runtime IP). What we configure

Speaker notes: Key insight: the Apigee runtime has no public IP — a PSC NEG (in the same region as the instance) privately connects it to the load balancer, which is the only exposed surface and is fronted by Cloud Armor. Decide early internet-facing vs internal (regional internal ALB) — it changes the LB type and DNS. Edge mTLS is configured on the target HTTPS proxy via a trust config.

Expert insight

Guardrails that commonly block provisioning

From the field — surface these before we start, or they become day-one blockers.

Org policy clashes

restrict-external-IP, require-CMEK, DRS and disable-SA-key constraints silently fail provisioning until exceptions are granted.

Overlapping CIDR

A /22 overlapping on-prem or a peered VPC can't be changed after peering — forces a costly re-provision. Validate against IPAM first.

VPC-SC perimeter

A strict perimeter blocks apigee & servicenetworking APIs without ingress/egress rules and access levels.

CMEK timing

Keys must exist in the correct region and grant the Apigee service agent access before provisioning — not after.

Missing IAM grants

Service-agent bindings (compute, servicenetworking, kms) are easy to miss and produce opaque permission errors.

On-prem long pole

Interconnect/VPN to reach backends can take weeks of lead time — start it in parallel, not at the end.

Speaker notes: Your credibility slide — it shows you've done this before. Walk each card and ask “does this apply to you?”, capturing an owner per item. The two that derail timelines most: overlapping CIDR and org-policy / VPC-SC exceptions.
