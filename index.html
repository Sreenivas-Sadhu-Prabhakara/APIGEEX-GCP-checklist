Concept

Apigee X in plain terms

Think of it as a managed front door for every digital service you expose — to partners, mobile apps, and internal teams.

API — a contract that lets one system safely use another's data or function.
Proxy — Apigee's managed “middleman” in front of each backend; you change the proxy, not the backend.
Gateway — the single, policed entry point where security, limits, and monitoring are enforced consistently.
Speaker notes: Use the “managed front door / reception desk” analogy. Reception checks ID (auth), enforces appointment limits (quotas/rate-limit), translates languages (mediation), and keeps a visitor log (analytics). Stress: backends don't have to change — the proxy absorbs that.

Business value

Why a managed gateway, not point-to-point

Without a gateway

  • ✗ Security re-implemented per app — inconsistent
  • ✗ No central view of who calls what
  • ✗ Backends exposed & brittle to change
  • ✗ Hard to throttle, monetize, or reuse APIs
  • ✗ Partner onboarding is bespoke & slow

With Apigee X

  • ✓ Consistent security — OAuth, API keys, mTLS, threat protection
  • ✓ Full visibility — analytics, latency, error & usage insight
  • ✓ Decoupling — evolve backends behind a stable contract
  • ✓ Control — quotas, spike arrest, caching, versioning
  • ✓ Velocity — self-service developer portal & reuse

What we configure

What we need from you

Speaker notes: The key insight: the Apigee runtime has no public IP — PSC privately connects it to the load balancer, which is the only thing exposed and is protected by Cloud Armor. Decide early whether ingress is internet-facing or internal (internal ALB) — it changes the LB type and DNS.

Expert insight

Guardrails that commonly block provisioning

From the field — surface these before we start, or they become day-one blockers.

Org policy clashes

Restrict-external-IP, require-CMEK, domain-restricted sharing, and disable-SA-key constraints silently fail provisioning until exceptions are granted.

Overlapping CIDR

A /22 that overlaps on-prem or a peered VPC can't be changed after peering — forces a costly re-provision. Validate against IPAM first.

VPC-SC perimeter

A strict perimeter blocks Apigee & service-networking APIs without ingress/egress rules and the right access levels.

CMEK timing

Keys must exist in the correct region and grant the Apigee service agent access before provisioning — not after.

Missing IAM grants

Service-agent role bindings (compute, servicenetworking, KMS) are easy to miss and produce opaque permission errors.

On-prem long pole

Interconnect/VPN to reach backends can take weeks of lead time — start it in parallel, not at the end.

Speaker notes: This is your credibility slide — it shows you've done this before. Walk each card briefly and ask “does any of this apply to you?” Capture owners against each. The two that derail timelines most: overlapping CIDR and org-policy/VPC-SC exceptions.

Sizing & cost

What drives sizing, regions & cost

Primary cost drivers

SubscriptionStd / Enterprise / Enterprise Plus tier, or pay-as-you-go API call volume.
InstancesEach region = one runtime instance. Multi-region for HA/DR multiplies cost.
EnvironmentsNumber of environments & entitlements vary by tier.
SurroundLoad balancer, Cloud Armor, NAT egress, KMS, logging/monitoring.

Sizing questions for you

  • ?Expected API call volume — steady & peak TPS
  • ?Latency / availability SLA targets
  • ?One region or multi-region (active-active)?
  • ?How many environments & teams onboarding?
  • ?Growth horizon — 6, 12, 24 months

Rule of thumb: start single-region for a pilot, design the network & env model for multi-region so scaling later is config, not a rebuild.

Speaker notes: Don't quote firm prices live — gather the inputs and follow up with a costed proposal. The cheapest mistake to avoid: under-planning the network so a later multi-region expansion forces a re-provision. Capture their volume & SLA numbers as the sizing basis.

De-risking

Risks & how we mitigate them

CIDR exhaustion / overlap

High

Mitigate: validate the /22 against IPAM in discovery; reserve before peering.

Org-policy / VPC-SC blocks

High

Mitigate: policy audit up front; pre-stage exceptions & ingress rules.

On-prem connectivity lead time

Medium

Mitigate: start Interconnect/VPN in week 1, parallel to provisioning.

Under-sizing / re-provision

Medium

Mitigate: design network & env model for multi-region from day one.

Unclear day-2 ownership

Medium

Mitigate: name platform owner & runbooks before go-live.

Input / approval delays

Manageable
